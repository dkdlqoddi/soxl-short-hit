Thu, November 5, 2026 at 8:45 PM UTC David Hollerith Retail traders dump $358 million in stocks in biggest selling day since 2020 Retail investors are dumping US stocks at a pace not seen since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, breaking with their typical market-down-day habit of "buying the dip." Everyday investors sold a net $358 million in individual US stocks on Thursday as of 2:58 p.m. ET, according to Vanda Research, putting them on track for their biggest single-day net selling spree since March 17, 2020, when retail investors pulled $502 million from individual stocks. "This is what we would call a total capitulation by retail investors," Viraj Patel, global macro strategist at Vanda Research, said in emailed comments. The selling marks a departure from retail investors' tendency to scoop up shares when markets tumble. Vanda said there were "no signs of buying the dip" in individual stocks, though some investors continued purchasing leveraged exchange-traded funds such as the ProShares UltraPro QQQ ( TQQQ ) and Direxion Daily Semiconductor Bull 3X Shares ( SOXL ). Patel added that the retreat echoed a broader "buyers' strike" among institutional investors alongside forced selling by systematic and leveraged investment strategies. Thu, November 5, 2026 at 7:25 PM UTC David Hollerith What is a correction? It's a bad day for the stock market. Major indexes are falling Thursday, with technology stocks leading the sell-off and semiconductor shares among the biggest decliners. But one rough session doesn't necessarily amount to what investors deem a correction. A correction generally refers to a decline of at least 10% from a recent high in a stock, index, or other asset. It is typically seen as a meaningful pullback, but one that stops short of a bear market, which is commonly defined as a drop of 20% or more. That distinction matters after Wednesday's sharp rally. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite ( ^IXIC ) and the narrower Nasdaq-100 are each on pace to close Thursday in correction territory, as is the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index ( ^SOX ).
